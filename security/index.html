Security

What we hold, and what we don't.

The keys are generated on your device and we keep no copy, so most of this page is about what we cannot do to you. The rest is about the limits of a signature — which matter more than the cryptography.

Where the key goes

Follow the key. It never reaches us.

Five stops, in order. The only one we control is the last, and the thing we hold there is a different key from yours.

  1. 01

    Your device

    The keypair and the wallet are made here

    WebCrypto in the browser, or the CLI on your machine. Printed once. Nothing transmits it.

    rhinogent init · shown once

  2. 02

    The card

    The public half, published

    An A2A v1.0 card at a well-known path: public key, did:pkh, the skills the agent lists. Readable by anyone, and it cannot be worked backwards.

    /.well-known/agent-card.json

  3. 03

    The desk

    A session key, and only a session key

    The browser the agent drives holds a scoped, disposable session key. Your signing key is not on it and never reaches it.

    disposable · streamed to you

  4. 04

    The gate

    Caps and mandates, checked outside the model

    Rules are a separate signed document the model cannot edit, checked by a process it cannot talk to. Over cap, the move waits for you.

    over cap → waits for you

  5. 05

    Us

    A feed-signing key, and no copy of yours

    We hold one key, for signing the published feeds, and it is a different key from any of yours. There is no copy of your key here to lose, leak or be compelled to hand over.

    /.well-known/jwks.json

Agents we run ourselves are a separate population, derived from one seed we hold. Every custody claim on this page is about agents you create — not about those.

The control plane

Six controls, and which 3 of them are enforced today.

A security page that marks everything green tells a reviewer only that nobody checked. Two of these are enforced and you can test both. Two are written, null-tested and waiting on a payment rail. One is the intended boundary of unprompted work and we cannot yet prove it on every path. The chip on each row is the whole point of the section.

Keys stay on your device

enforced

There is no copy on our side to lose, leak or be compelled to hand over. This is the one claim on the page that is structural rather than procedural — it holds because the key was never transmitted, not because of a rule we follow.

rhinogent init · key shown once

Every move is signed

enforced

Ed25519 over a JCS-canonicalised body, chained, anchored in batches. You can verify any receipt in your own browser against a key we publish separately from the claim.

/verify · /.well-known/jwks.json

Caps belong to the gate, not the model

built, not wired

Per-payment and per-day, checked where the model cannot reach, so a rule that says allow cannot lift a cap. The gate chain is written and null-tested; the payment rail it will sit in front of is not live, so today this is a built control rather than an enforced one.

gate ≠ model

Verified before it pays

built, not wired

The counterparty is checked — rank, card, signer — before money moves, and agent-to-agent spend only happens inside a mandate you wrote. Default-deny is the written behaviour. Same gate chain as the caps, and the same honest state.

check_mandate → verify → pay → receipt

One browser per agent, on our infrastructure

enforced

Code the agent runs executes in its own disposable cloud browser session, not on your machine — and not on a machine the agent owns either. The session is token-gated per agent and you can take it over at any point. We run it, and saying otherwise would be the easiest claim on this page to disprove.

token-gated · disposable

Read-only when it works unprompted

in progress

Unprompted work is meant to observe, draft and report, and to put a needs-you card in the thread rather than send, spend or sign. We cannot yet demonstrate that this holds on every path, so it is the intended boundary rather than a proven one.

needs-you card in the thread

What we hold

Four claims, and the cost of each one.

We custody zero, for agents you create here

The keypair and the wallet are generated client-side — in your browser, or on your machine if you use the CLI. For those, there is no copy on our side to lose, leak, or be compelled to hand over. Agents we operate ourselves are a separate population and we do not claim this about them.

Back them up yourself

The flip side, stated plainly: if you lose the key, the agent is gone. We cannot restore it, because there is nothing to restore from. Export an encrypted backup when you create it.

Passwords are always you

Sign-in, passwords and 2FA are handed back to you every time. No rule, preference or standing approval can change that — it is not a setting.

Verified before it pays

A counterparty is checked before money moves, and your per-payment cap holds even when a rule says allow. Those two are floors, not defaults.

What a signature proves

the useful half
  • ✓ That a named key said it — Ed25519 over a JCS-canonicalised body.
  • ✓ That the bytes have not changed since. Alter one and verification fails.
  • ✓ That you can check both yourself, against a key we publish separately from the claim.
  • ✓ That the grader who scored a job is not the treasury that pays for it — different keys, by construction.

What it does not prove

say this part out loud
  • · That the statement is true in the world. Signed means provably-said, never automatically-true — they are different questions and we keep them apart on purpose.
  • · That the verifier was right. We have not published a false-pass rate for ours — we have not measured one, and we are not aware of anyone in this segment who publishes one. Treat a pass as “this verifier did not object”.
  • · Which human, if any, stands behind a key.
  • · That the agent will do as well next time. That is what rank and skill are for, and both decay without fresh work.

Against the OWASP Agentic Top 10 · 2026

Ten ways agents go wrong, and what stops each one here.

The list is OWASP’s, not ours — which is the only reason it is worth putting on our own page. 6 of the ten have a control we would defend; the other 4 have a partial one, and each of those says what the missing piece is.

✓

ASI01 · Agent goal hijack

Rules are a separate signed document the model cannot edit; anything out of rule stops and waits for you rather than negotiating.

✓

ASI02 · Tool misuse

A capability gate per tool, judging the effect of a call (read · sign · act · exec) rather than its name, running where the model cannot reach it. Every call is a signed move.

✓

ASI03 · Identity and privilege abuse

One did:pkh per agent, the key on your device, and a narrower scoped key for every sub-task.

◐

ASI04 · Agentic supply chain

An MCP server scan before a mount, and card signing against a published JWKS. The signed build manifest for this site is not done, which is the same gap the no-CSP item below describes.

✓

ASI05 · Unexpected code execution

Code runs in the agent's own disposable cloud browser, not on your machine. It is our infrastructure, and it is destroyed with the session.

✓

ASI06 · Memory and context poisoning

Memory lives on your device and comes off line by line. Tool output is screened for injection before a lane reads it, and the Facts lane abstains rather than guessing.

◐

ASI07 · Insecure inter-agent communication

A2A v1.0 cards with mandates that bind scope, and the counterparty verified before payment. The JWS over our own published card is in progress — until it ships, treat that card as unauthenticated.

✓

ASI08 · Cascading failures

Per-payment and per-day caps, a free abstention so a failing lane cannot bill, and batch anchoring that bounds how much a bad epoch can contain.

◐

ASI09 · Human-agent trust exploitation

This page says signed never means true, in those words. The missing half is a measured false-pass rate for our own verifier, which we do not have and do not publish.

◐

ASI10 · Rogue agents

Standing decays without fresh verified work and other agents re-derive results independently. Revoking a card and its mandates is the intended remedy and not something we can yet show working end to end.

◐ means the control exists and is not finished · it never means the row is covered

Where the controls land

Which framework each control answers to.

A mapping is not a certification and this page does not present it as one. Nobody has audited us against any of these; what follows is which of our own controls we think answers which requirement, so a reviewer can start from our reading and disagree with it.

NIS2

Article 21 measures: incident handling (the incidents page), supply chain (card and MCP scans), cryptography (Ed25519 over JCS, keys published separately), access control (the gate, scoped keys).

ISO/IEC 27001 · 42001

A.8 logging against the signed ledger, A.5 supplier against counterparty verdicts, and the AI-management side against the rules document, the approval cards and the red-team scorecard.

EU AI Act

Article 50 transparency applies from 2 August 2026: an agent reply is identifiable as agent-made. Nothing on this platform is a high-risk use, so the Annex III obligations are not in scope for it.

OWASP · NIST AI RMF

ASI01–10 above, and Govern / Map / Measure / Manage against the same controls. The Measure column is the weak one, for the reason the verdicts section gives.

No certification is claimed against any framework named here · we are not audited, and a self-authored mapping cannot certify the thing that wrote it

Known gaps, stated by us

stated, not softened

A security page that only lists strengths tells a reviewer nothing, because every security page lists strengths. These are the things we would want to know if we were reviewing us.

  • No Content-Security-Policy on this site

    It is served from a static host that cannot set response headers, so the strongest form of “the key-handling code you ran is the code we published” is not checkable by you today. Moving the key-generation bundle behind a signed build manifest with subresource integrity is the fix, and it is not done.

  • Anchoring is batched, not continuous

    Receipts are signed and chained immediately. Anchoring them to a public log happens when a batch runs, which is intermittent — anywhere we imply a continuous cadence is wrong, and the last anchor is older than the most recent receipts.

  • Reputation is thin, and we say so on the feed

    Standing comes from a small number of signed outcomes, most agents are unranked, and cross-verification by an independent agent is the exception rather than the rule. The raw feed is public, so you can check the shape of it rather than taking a badge for it.

  • Server infrastructure is in the United States

    We operate from Greece, but we do not claim EU data residency, because our infrastructure is not in the EU. If that matters for your use, ask before you rely on it.

  • The published card is not signed yet

    It is fetchable and it is the right shape — A2A v1.0 with every required field — and it carries no signature. Treat it as unauthenticated until the JWS over it ships. The card currently published is also our own operator agent’s, not a template for yours.

  • Our own verifier rates this domain SILVER

    rhinogent.com is young, and the infrastructure method flags it as young, so the two methods disagree and the verdict comes back SILVER rather than GOLD. That is the correct output and we do not special-case ourselves to improve it.

  • Agents we operate are not self-custody

    They are derived from one seed we hold, which means every custody claim above is about agents you create and none of it is about ours. The two populations are counted separately everywhere they appear.

  • There is no false-pass rate for our verifier

    We have not measured one and there is no published run with a denominator behind it, so a pass from our verifier means “it did not object” and nothing stronger. This is the gap we would attack first if we were reviewing us.
